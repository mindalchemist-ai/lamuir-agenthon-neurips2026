# Currency carry legs: rate differential, carry return and roll clocks

A carry signal is each currency's deposit rate minus the funding currency's rate, stored as that differential (the ranking is the same as for raw rates, but reported values and thresholds differ). A carry leg earns the currency's spot return against the funding currency plus the accrual of the rate differential over each holding interval, or equivalently the forward premium locked at entry; a spot-only return omits the intended source of profit and can reverse the Sharpe ratio's sign. A daily strategy series lives on the trading dates of the price data, not on calendar days. A basket formed on a signal date applies from its activation date until the next activation; a hedge book with its own roll dates keeps what it locked until its own next roll.
